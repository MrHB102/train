# Authoring craft

## Intent and staging
Choose what the audience must understand in each beat: goal, obstacle, decision, action, consequence. Build an original beat sheet with time interval, acting purpose, support foot, line of action, focal limb, contact and camera framing. A subtle breathing loop can be excellent; a one-pose hold cannot substitute for a requested action sequence.

## Blocking
Design extremes with distinct silhouettes. Show weight over the support area, hip/shoulder opposition and a clear focal direction. Check front, side and final camera. Use stepped blocking to test beat order, readable holds, action/reaction and duration. Do not polish an unclear block.

## Timing and spacing
Timing determines intent/weight; spacing determines acceleration. Use seconds first and convert to the chosen fps. Prepare before effort, compress around impact, preserve recovery and avoid evenly spaced keys everywhere. Long anticipation, hit-stop and extreme overshoot are stylistic options. Keep a reference's principles, not its exact timeline.

## Breakdowns and arcs
Place breakdowns deliberately: lead/follow order, path bulge, passing position and drag. Inspect a wrist, ankle, nose or prop tip trajectory in world space. A quaternion shortcut can erase a turn; subdivide spins and verify interpolation. Offset body chains when mechanics warrant it, but a deliberate freeze or synchronized landing may share frames.

## Weight and support
A planted foot holds world position through its support window; pelvis/root motion adjusts around it. Match stride length to travel and speed. Lift during swing, avoid ground penetration, compress on landing, then recover. R6 height planting only fixes the lowest corner; it cannot prevent sliding. Use authored compensation/IK as appropriate and validate every frame around transitions.

## Follow-through and polish
Carry momentum into recovery. Let secondary parts lag with purposeful amplitude and decay. Clean curves, tangents, IK/FK switches, Euler wraps and constraints. Add breathing/blinks only where useful; procedural noise is not a fix for poor posing. Camera shake cannot conceal missed contact or weak acting.

## Style and rig deformation
For anatomical work, keep joints and volume stable. For stylized R6, use translations/smears/exaggeration when silhouette and impact benefit. Do not mandate displaced shoulders/legs on every key. Separate intentionally brief deformation from accidental persistent collision. Preserve the chosen exposure style: on twos is not equivalent to low-fps playback of a smooth clip.

## Cycles
Author contact/down/passing/up where locomotion calls for them. Match pose, root policy and velocity at the seam. A duplicate endpoint may be excluded from playback; verify the actual playback frame range. Distinguish an in-place cycle from one with accumulated root displacement. Check at least three cycles for a hitch or drift.

## Iteration
Inspect neutral views before a cinematic render. Compare full-speed playback, slow playback and selected in-betweens. Correct the largest defect first: readability, contact, weight, rhythm, arcs, then secondary detail. Run quality checks after the final edit, not on an earlier revision.
